Multimodal Mamba Fusion for Noise-Robust EEG Biometric Identification

ICML Submitted: April 28, 2026

Summary

This paper proposes a two-stage EEG biometric pipeline that augments a WaveNet denoiser with a selective state-space (Mamba) block (Stage I) and introduces a multimodal Stage II that fuses a ResNet-based waveform branch with a spectrogram branch processed by Mamba sweeps via a cross-attention fusion module. Evaluated on the PhysioNet Auditory-Evoked-Potential dataset under a held-out-subject open-set protocol (for verification) and a session-disjoint closed-set retrieval protocol, the full multimodal model (V4) achieves the best reported Precision@1 across three synthetic noise families, while open-set verification performance remains near chance (AUROC ≈ 0.50), which the authors candidly flag as an open problem.

Strengths

  • Technical novelty and innovation
    • Combines a Mamba-augmented WaveNet denoiser with a spectrogram Mamba branch and cross-attention fusion; the dual “time-domain + time-frequency” modeling with selective state-space sweeps is a thoughtful multimodal design.
    • The cross-attention gate over two normalized branch embeddings is a simple but potentially effective alternative to naive concatenation or late fusion.
    • Auxiliary branch losses to discourage fusion collapse are a practical and well-motivated training detail.
  • Experimental rigor and validation
    • Clear four-version comparison (V1–V4) under consistent splits and seeds isolates contributions of Mamba in Stage I and the multimodal fusion in Stage II.
    • Reports both retrieval (P@1/P@5) and open-set verification (AUROC/EER) along with SI-SNR for the enhancement stage; latency and parameter counts are provided.
    • Multiple metric-learning heads (ArcFace, Multi-Similarity) and backbones (ResNet-18/34) are explored, with mean±std across seeds for V4.
  • Clarity of presentation
    • The two-stage pipeline and architectural choices are described in sufficient detail to follow; hyperparameters, shapes, and training schedules are specified.
    • The paper is forthright about the verification gap and about the limited effect of a single Mamba block inside the denoiser.
  • Significance of contributions
    • Addresses the important problem of noise robustness for EEG biometrics and explicitly probes the tension between closed-set retrieval and open-set verification.
    • Provides an engineering recipe that others working on resource-constrained biosignal systems may adapt (e.g., time-frequency Mamba sweeps and lightweight fusion).

Weaknesses

  • Technical limitations or concerns
    • The absolute retrieval gains over the WaveNet-only baseline are small (≤1.7 points P@1 on two of three noise types), and the single Mamba insertion into Stage I does not bring consistent benefits.
    • Using cross-attention over only two tokens is arguably overkill; an ablation against simpler fusion (concatenation + MLP or gated averaging without attention) is missing.
    • The spectrogram branch relies on a fixed STFT; alternatives (learned filterbanks, wavelets, multi-resolution) are not considered.
  • Experimental gaps or methodological issues
    • P@5 is reported lower than P@1 in Table II, which violates metric monotonicity and suggests a definition or implementation error.
    • The dataset is very small (20 subjects, 4 channels), with only 4 held-out “unknown” identities for open-set verification; the reported AUROC ≈ 0.50 on unknowns heavily limits claims about generalizable verification.
    • Noise robustness is assessed with synthetic noise injected at test time; no evaluation on real artifacts or cross-session/device nonstationarities beyond what the dataset natively offers.
    • No comparison to strong modern EEG biometric baselines on open-set verification (e.g., recent large-scale metric learning pipelines), nor to spectrogram-only or EEG-only variants within V4 to quantify fusion benefits directly.
    • Stage I benefit attribution is unclear: different versions retrain Stage I independently, making SI-SNR deltas hard to interpret as architectural effects rather than run-to-run variance.
  • Clarity or presentation issues
    • The retrieval vs verification protocol is unconventional and could be explained more clearly (e.g., how prototypes are formed, exact enrollment/verification splits for known vs unknown subjects).
    • Important ablation details are missing: impact of auxiliary losses λe/λs, removing cross-attention, using only one Mamba sweep (time or frequency), or replacing Mamba with lightweight Transformers/TCNs.
  • Missing related work or comparisons
    • Recent large-scale EEG biometric evaluations (e.g., PEERS-based metric learning with strong open-set results and permanence analyses) are not discussed or used as context/baselines.
    • Comparisons to Transformer/S4-style models for the spectrogram branch or to widely used EEG backbones (EEGNet/DeepConvNet) under the same protocol are absent.

Detailed Comments

  • Technical soundness evaluation
    • The architectural components are reasonable and internally consistent; the SI-SNR objective for denoising is standard and the metric-learning heads are appropriate for open-set scenarios.
    • However, the limited effect of inserting a single Mamba block into WaveNet suggests that more careful placement/depth or replacing larger portions of the stack might be necessary to realize SSM advantages; as-is, the V2 experiment mostly shows that a single block swap does not help.
    • The cross-attention over two tokens will reduce to content-dependent mixing, similar to a gated fusion layer. Without a direct ablation to simpler fusion, it is hard to justify the added complexity.
  • Experimental evaluation assessment
    • The hybrid evaluation separating closed-set retrieval (on seen identities, session-disjoint) from open-set verification (on held-out subjects) is thoughtful, but the dataset scale limits conclusions about the latter. AUROC near 0.5 with EER around 0.35–0.41 on only four unknown subjects indicates that the system, in its current form, is not viable for verification.
    • The P@5 anomaly (lower than P@1) is a red flag indicating either a metric definition or coding issue; this must be resolved to trust the retrieval results.
    • Reported improvements are modest and confidence intervals overlap substantially (e.g., Gaussian P@1 0.824±0.036 for V4 vs V1 best 0.822), suggesting gains may be within noise.
    • The reliance on synthetic noise for both training and evaluation provides limited evidence about robustness to real-world artifacts (blink/EMG/motion) and nonstationarities that are central to wearables.
    • Missing ablations significantly weaken claims: no spectrogram-only vs EEG-only comparisons in V4; no removal of auxiliary losses; no replacement of cross-attention with simpler pooling; no swap of Mamba with a lightweight Transformer/S4 for the 2D branch.
  • Comparison with related work (using the summaries provided)
    • The paper positions Mamba appropriately relative to SSMs and notes its efficiency. However, it does not connect to recent large-scale EEG biometric studies that emphasize open-set verification, longitudinal permanence, and cross-device/domain generalization (e.g., the PEERS benchmark with metric learning), which would contextualize the near-chance AUROC here and suggest remedies (e.g., enrollment reinforcement, multi-sample verification, calibration strategies).
    • Prior work on EEG classification with WaveNet/TCN backbones and on compact EEG architectures (EEGNet/DeepConvNet) should be included in empirical comparisons or at least in discussion under the same protocol to establish that the proposed fusion appreciably advances the state of the art.
    • In the time-frequency modeling space, alternatives such as Audio Spectrogram Transformers or ConvNeXt/ResNet backbones on spectrograms should be considered as baselines; citing AST is good, but comparative results are missing.
  • Discussion of broader impact and significance
    • EEG biometrics have high potential impact but also significant privacy and ethical considerations; brief discussion of data protection, consent, and risks of misuse/spoofing would strengthen the paper.
    • The real-time compatibility claim is useful for practical deployment; however, without robust open-set verification and validation on larger, more diverse cohorts, deployment-readiness remains far off.
    • The candid reporting of the verification gap is commendable; the follow-up directions mentioned (calibration, prototype-aware logits, adversarial negatives) are appropriate and should be prioritized, ideally with preliminary results.

Questions for Authors

  1. How exactly are enrollment prototypes constructed for verification? Which sessions are used for prototype creation versus verification, and how do you prevent leakage between retrieval and verification splits?
  2. Why is P@5 consistently lower than P@1 in Table II? Please clarify the definition and computation of P@K and correct any implementation issues.
  3. Can you report ablations for fusion: (i) concatenation + MLP, (ii) simple gated averaging without attention, (iii) no auxiliary branch losses, and (iv) spectrogram-only and EEG-only variants within V4?
  4. For the spectrogram branch, why choose fixed STFT parameters (nfft=128, hop=64)? Did you try multi-resolution features, learnable filterbanks, or wavelets, and how sensitive are results to these choices?
  5. Can you provide an apples-to-apples comparison against EEGNet/DeepConvNet and a simple Transformer/S4 on the spectrogram branch under the same protocol?
  6. How many training subjects remain after holding out {2,5,7,12}, and how many samples per subject are used for enrollment and verification? Please include ROC/DET curves and calibration analyses (e.g., score distributions per subject) to visualize the verification gap.
  7. Do your conclusions hold without synthetic noise at test time (i.e., on the raw dataset only)? Please add a “no added noise” condition to assess real-world robustness.
  8. What justifies inserting only a single Mamba block in Stage I? Did you experiment with deeper SSM substitution or placing blocks at different layers? Please include SI-SNR vs P@1 tradeoffs and training stability observations.
  9. Are the observed V4 gains statistically significant over V1 under your three-seed setup? Please provide confidence intervals or hypothesis tests.
  10. Could you release the exact code and metrics computation scripts to facilitate reproduction and validation of P@K and AUROC/EER?

Overall Assessment

The paper tackles an important and timely problem—noise-robust EEG biometrics—and proposes a reasonably novel multimodal architecture that combines Mamba-augmented denoising and spectrogram-based SSM processing with gated fusion. The implementation details are clear, the engineering is careful, and the authors are commendably transparent about limitations, especially the verification gap. However, the empirical gains over a strong WaveNet-only baseline are modest; key ablations and baseline comparisons are missing; the dataset scale is small with only four unknown subjects for open-set verification; and a fundamental metric inconsistency (P@5 < P@1) undermines confidence in the retrieval results. As a result, while the approach is promising, the current evidence is insufficient for ICML. I recommend rejection in its current form, with a clear path to strengthening: fix evaluation issues; provide stronger, statistically grounded ablations and baselines (including spectrogram-only and simpler fusion); evaluate on larger and more realistic corpora (e.g., PEERS) with robust open-set protocols and calibration; and assess real artifact robustness without synthetic perturbations. If these are addressed, the work could make a solid contribution to the community.
