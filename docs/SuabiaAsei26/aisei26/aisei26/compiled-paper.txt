                                                                         Noise synthesis and denoiser training. To train Stage I, we
                                                                         construct paired samples (xnoisy , xclean ) using only subjects in

                                                                         and generate xnoisy = xclean + αn, where n is sampled from
                                                                         Gaussian / power-line / EMG noise and α is set to match
                                                                         SNR levels in {0, 5, 10, 20} dB. Care is taken to ensure no
                                                                         information leakage between training and evaluation splits
                                                                         during noise mixing. The WaveNet denoiser is optimized with
                                                                         the SI-SNR objective to recover xclean from xnoisy .

                                                                         B. Stage II: Discriminative Representation Learning
                                                                            In the second stage, denoised signals are processed by a
                                                                         deep metric learning model to learn identity-discriminative
                                                                         embeddings [5], [6]. Residual convolutional neural networks
                                                                         (ResNet) are used as backbone architectures due to their
                                                                         stability and strong representation capacity [11]. Angular
                                                                         margin–based loss functions are employed to enforce compact
                                                                         intra-class clustering and clear inter-class separation in the
                                                                         embedding space [7]. In addition, general pair-weighted metric
                                                                         objectives (e.g., multi-similarity loss) can further improve
                                                                         embedding discrimination by leveraging multiple positive and
                                                                         negative relationships within a batch [8]. This embedding-
                                                                         based formulation naturally supports open-set recognition,
                                                                         enabling the detection of unseen users based on distance to
                                                                         learned identity prototypes in the embedding space [5], [6].
